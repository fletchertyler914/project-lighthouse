**Subject:** Energy-conserving shock relations, Prandtl–Meyer flow and a vorticity–entropy relation on leaves of
Riemann's Nachlass (Cod. Ms. B. Riemann 26 fol. 70–71 and Cod. Ms. B. Riemann 19 fol. 3v)

Dear Professor Darrigol,

While reading the digitized Riemann Nachlass at SUB Göttingen, I came across sheets which seem to bear on the
history of gas dynamics. They are in Riemann's notation of 1856–60 (p = φ(ρ), ∫φ'(ρ) d log ρ, "aa" for a², k for
the adiabatic exponent, 2r = f + u) and contain, as far as I can read them:

1. Cod. Ms. B. Riemann 26 ("Mathematische Notizen", 1853–1863; GDZ DE-611-HS-3206190), fol. 70r: an energy balance
   across a moving discontinuity for a polytropic gas, leading to p₁ − p₂ = v₁ρ₁(v₂ − v₁), p₁ + p₂ = (1/k)v₁ρ₁(v₁ + v₂)
   and 2p₂/ρ₂ + v₂²(1 − 1/k) = v₁v₂(1 + 1/k) = 2p₁/ρ₁ + v₁²(1 − 1/k) — i.e. the energy-conserving normal-shock
   relations and the relation v₁v₂ = c*² usually attributed to Prandtl (1906).
   Scan: https://images.sub.uni-goettingen.de/iiif/image/gdz:DE-611-HS-3206190:00000141/full/1600,/0/default.jpg
2. fol. 70v: a Bernoulli relation along streamlines and the line (1/ρ)(∂v/∂x − ∂u/∂y) = −1/(k−1) ∂p/∂σ, with σ the
   stream function and p = ρ^k g(σ) — the vorticity behind a curved shock expressed by the cross-streamline entropy
   gradient (the relation now called Crocco's theorem, 1937), followed by a stream-function equation for rotational
   compressible flow with the entropy term.
   Scan: …:00000142/…
3. fol. 71r: steady two-dimensional supersonic flow with the invariant 2r = σ + λ·arctan(y/λ) − arctan(y),
   y = √(M² − 1), λ² = (k+1)/(k−1) — the Prandtl–Meyer function — a centred simple wave, and a sketch of rays
   fanning from the corner of a channel; fol. 71v: the Mach-line directions (u ∓ v√(M²−1), v ± u√(M²−1)) and a
   characteristic-coordinate form of the equations.
   Scans: …:00000143/… and …:00000144/…
4. Cod. Ms. B. Riemann 19 ("Abel'sche Functionen und Theta", 1850–1865; GDZ DE-611-HS-3206670), fol. 3v: oblique
   shocks with the energy-conserving relation a² − b²sin²φ = λ²·b cosφ·c cosψ, a shock-polar-type parametrisation
   and the hypersonic maximum deflection (sin α_max = 1/k in modern terms), with a wedge sketch. The recto, fol. 3r,
   carries theta-function calculations belonging to the April-1858 work of fol. 1r, and also a marginal note in
   Italian ("Ma se ha finito, deve pagare per tutto il tempo. Ciò andrebbe regolarizzato"), so the leaf was in use
   both in 1858 and during Riemann's Italian years (1862–66).
   Scan: https://images.sub.uni-goettingen.de/iiif/image/gdz:DE-611-HS-3206670:00000008/full/1600,/0/default.jpg

I have checked the algebra by computer (the relations hold under conservation of mass, momentum and energy and fail
for the isentropic jump of Riemann's 1860 paper; the Prandtl–Meyer invariant and the Mach-angle directions are
exact). I could not find these leaves discussed anywhere: Weber's 1901 edition of the lectures still defends the
isentropic treatment, and the 1902 Nachträge report on these folders mentions no gas dynamics. A comparison of the
handwriting with Riemann's dated air-wave drafts of 1856–57 (Cod. Ms. B. Riemann 21) and with the catalogued hands
of Weber and Weierstraß in Riemann 19 suggests Riemann's hand, but I am not a palaeographer.

Three things would settle the matter, and I would be very grateful for your view or for a pointer to the right
person:
- whether fol. 3v is the same hand and ink campaign as fol. 3r (certainly Riemann's, April 1858);
- whether three specific lines read as above on the originals: the "(1 + 1/k)" line on fol. 70r/v, the
  cross-streamline term ∂p/∂σ on fol. 70v, and the v₁v₂ parametrisation on fol. 3v;
- whether the paper of fol. 70–71 matches the 1850s–60s stock of the dated sheets in Riemann 21.

If the hand and date (before July 1866) are confirmed, the sheets would contain energy-conserving shock relations
twelve years before Rankine, together with results usually dated to 1906–1937.
