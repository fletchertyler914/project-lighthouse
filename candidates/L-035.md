## Step 1: human read of the pages
Read on the scans 2026-11-09; the transcriptions match.

**(a) Fol. 19/16v**, below notes on theta characteristics, a letter draft, signed "B. Riemann":
`Hochgeehrter Herr Ober-Medicinalrath. Für die bei der nächsten öffentlichen Sitzung zu stellende Preisaufgabe erlaube
ich mir folgendes Thema vorzuschlagen. In tabulis numerorum primorum quousque pergitur, semper numeri primi
inveniuntur, quorum differentia = 2, unde valde verisimile est, seriem horum numerorum nunquam abrumpi. Jam postulat
Soc. R. literarum, ut argumentis firmis dijudicetur, utrum multitudo binorum numerorum primorum duabus tantum
unitatibus differentium in infinitum crescat nec ne.` Then the German version: `In den Primzahltafeln findet man, wie
weit man auch fortgehen möge, immer noch Primzahlen, deren Differenz = 2 ist und es ist daher sehr wahrscheinlich, daß
die Reihe dieser Zahlen niemals abbricht. Die K. S. der Wissenschaften verlangt nun, daß durch strenge Schlüsse
entschieden werde, ob die Anzahl Primzahlen, die um zwei Einheiten verschieden sind, unendlich ist oder nicht.`
The Society's secretary at the time was the chemist Wöhler, an Obermedicinalrath.
Printed version, read on the OCR of the Nachrichten 1861 (archive.org `nachrichtengott1861`), p. 337: "Für den November
1864 macht die K. Societät die folgende, von der mathematischen Classe gestellte Preisfrage bekannt: In tabulis
numerorum primorum, quousque pergitur, semper inveniuntur numeri primi, quorum differentia est = 2; unde valde
verisimile est seriem horum numerorum nunquam abrumpi. Jam postulat Soc. R. 'ut argumentis firmis dijudicetur, utrum
multitudo binorum numerorum primorum duobus tantum unitatibus differentium sit infinita necne'." The wording is the
draft's, with small changes. Outcome, Nachrichten 1864, pp. 371–372: two entries, no prize.

**(b) Fol. 18/29r**, verbatim:
`φ(g, x) = Anzahl der Primzahlen kleiner als x, [deren] nächstfolgende um g grösser sind.`
`∫∂x/log x = φ(2,x) + φ(4,x) + φ(6,x) + …` · `x = 2φ(2,x) + 4φ(4,x) + 6φ(6,x)` ·
`∫∂x/log x = ½∫φ(g,x)∂g` · `x = ½∫φ(g,x) g ∂g` · `φ'(g,x)/φ(g,x) = −(∫∂x/log x)/x` ·
`log φ(g,x) = −g (∫∂x/log x)/x` · `φ(g,x) = C e^{−g(∫∂x/log x)/x} = (2(∫∂x/log x)²/x) e^{−g(∫∂x/log x)/x}`.
Gloss: φ(g, x) counts the primes below x whose next prime is exactly g larger. Every prime has one successor and
the gaps add up to x. Assuming the count falls off at a constant relative rate in g, the count is
2·Li(x)²/x · exp(−g·Li(x)/x): an exponential (Poisson-type) law for prime gaps with mean x/Li(x) ≈ log x.
The page never sets g = 2; doing so gives about 2x/(log x)² twin primes below x.
Above it on the page: Mellin-type lemmas; below it: the asymptotic series of Li(x). Facing page 28v: Π(s−1)ζ(s) as an
integral, the functional equation, log ζ = −Σ log(1 − p^{−s}).

**Same words on both pages.** "Anzahl", "Primzahlen" and "um … sind" occur in the German of both. Crops anyone can open:
https://images.sub.uni-goettingen.de/iiif/image/gdz:DE-611-HS-3206649:00000057/pct:41.1,16.2,58.3,7.7/full/0/default.jpg (29r) and

## Step 2: maths checked by code
`verify/r18_29r_prime_gap_law.py`: the constant C = 2Li²/x and the second moment follow from the two counting
identities once the exponential form is assumed (sympy). Against the real primes:

| x | gaps 2–30, counted / law | gaps 32–60 | gaps 62–90 | twin primes: counted / law at g = 2 / Hardy–Littlewood 1923 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10⁶ | 1.13 | 0.67 | 0.32 | 8,169 / 10,565 / 8,248 |
| 10⁷ | 1.12 | 0.81 | 0.50 | 58,980 / 77,413 / 58,754 |
| 10⁸ | 1.11 | 0.89 | 0.62 | 440,312 / 591,777 / 440,368 |

The law has the right shape and scale and improves with x. It ignores the arithmetic factor that depends on the prime
factors of g (single gaps are off by 0.7–1.9×; its twin-prime constant is 2 where Hardy–Littlewood have 1.32), and its
tail is too heavy at reachable x.

## Step 3: literature search on the meaning
Independent agent, sources read, 2026-11-09, plus two further web searches by a maintainer.
**Is either page reported? Not found.** Read: Werke 1892 and Nachträge 1902 (every "Primzahl" hit is the 1859 paper,
its notes or the biography; the editors' report on Akt 18 says only "Naturphilosophisches … enthält auch noch Blätter
zu XII der Werke" and mentions only a hydrodynamic prize proposal); Siegel 1932; all 55 arXiv abstracts of Arias de
Reyna and three papers in full; Gabcke (arXiv:1512.02976); Dickson, History I ch. XVIII (no mention of the prize
question); the twin-prime history in arXiv:1205.0774, which says that after Polignac "it was 30 years before the
subject was revisited" (Glaisher 1878); nine web searches in German and English; archive.org full-text searches.
The published 1859 paper itself says of the local thickening and thinning of the primes "ohne dass jedoch hierin eine
Gesetzmässigkeit bemerkt worden wäre".
**Who first stated the gap law?** Polignac 1849: existence of every even difference, no law. Gauss to Encke 1849:
counts per hundred, no law. Sylvester 1871 (via Dickson): π(n)²/n for Goldbach counts, the same shape as the page's
prefactor. Glaisher 1877–78: enumerations. Hardy–Littlewood 1923: pairs differing by k, not consecutive primes.
Western 1934: a table of maximal gaps. Cramér 1936: the random model (credited by Soundararajan 2006; original not
read). Cherwell 1946 (Quart. J. Math. Oxford 17, pp. 56–57): the first explicit "uniform exponential decrease" for
the frequency of neighbouring-prime intervals found in print (so also Wolf, arXiv:1102.0481). Gallagher 1976: the
exponential law, conditional on the Hardy–Littlewood k-tuple conjecture. First quantitative twin-prime estimate
found: Brun 1915, then Hardy–Littlewood 1923.
**Not accessed:** Edwards 1974, Narkiewicz 2000, Laugwitz, Bottazzini–Gray, Neuenschwander 1988, Granville 1995,
Pintz 2007, Cramér 1936, Stäckel 1916–18, Dedekind's Werke and Nachlass, the Society's own archive.

## Verdict
LEAD, in two parts.
(a) **Established on the scan and the 1861 print:** Riemann proposed the twin-prime problem as the Göttingen Society's
prize question for 1864. No source found credits him. A notable historical fact (about 5/10); it needs no hand test.
(b) **Conditional: 6/10 if fol. 29r is in Riemann's hand, ceiling 7.** A private exponential law for prime gaps some
75 years before the first one found in print. Not usable mathematics today: an ansatz without an argument, without
the arithmetic factor.
Open: palaeographic confirmation of 29r (Riemann or Dedekind); the sources not accessed, above all Narkiewicz and
Laugwitz; the Society's archive for the fair copy of the letter.
