## Step 1 — human read of the pages
Read on the scans; transcriptions match. Content (ledger entry L-019 has the verbatim lines): on 70r the jump
relations across a discontinuity including the energy (not isothermal/adiabatic-Poisson) condition; on 71r the
turning-angle function of an expanding supersonic stream with a sketch; on 19/3v the oblique-shock relations with a
wedge sketch and the Mach-line construction; on 70v/71v the relation dp/ρ + ω dω = −(1/(k−1)) d_σp/ρ (vorticity–entropy
type) and the characteristic angle sin μ = 1/M.

## Step 2 — maths checked by code
All four scripts pass (sympy): the jump relations are the Rankine–Hugoniot conditions with energy conservation;
the 71r integral is the Prandtl–Meyer function; the 3v relations are the oblique-shock relations; the 70v/71v
identities hold for p = ρ^k g(σ) with uniform total enthalpy; sin μ = 1/M.

## Step 3 — literature search on the meaning
Riemann 1860 published the isothermal/adiabatic version famous for getting the energy condition wrong (Rayleigh 1910;
Rankine 1870; Hugoniot 1887). Nothing in the Werke, Weber's notes or the Nachträge 1902 reports prints these sheets.
Prandtl 1906 (relation), Meyer 1908 (function), Crocco 1937 (Friedmann 1922). The Mach angle sin μ = 1/M is Mach &
Salcher 1887; the characteristics method built on it is Prandtl–Busemann 1929. No source earlier than the date bracket
found for the shock, Prandtl, Prandtl–Meyer, oblique-shock and vorticity items.

## Re-check (2026-11-09, second pass over the whole record)
- **Scans re-read line by line:** 70v, 71r, 71v, 19/3v and 19/3r against the transcriptions; they match. (70r was not
  re-read in this pass; its decisive line is repeated as the first line of 70v.) No anachronistic symbol on any page
  (no M, c*, κ, γ; squares are written aa, λλ, yy, bb; "tg", "const.").
- **Correction, sketch on 71r:** it is not a fan from one corner. It shows the end of a channel; faint lines from its
  two corners meet at a point on the axis, where two heavy lines cross, with the sectors labelled ψ and π/2 (the
  limiting case tg ϱ = λ, tg ψ = 1/λ worked out beside it). Two waves crossing in front of a channel mouth is also the
  jet pattern photographed by Mach & Salcher (1889) and treated by Prandtl (1904), so the content alone cannot date
  the sheet; the hand, the paper and the leaf 19/3 have to.
- **Correction, Italian note on 19/3r:** it reads "Ma, se ha fissato, deve pagare per tutto il tempo. Può andare, se
  gli piace" (pencil, vertical in the right margin). The earlier reading ("finito … Ciò andrebbe regolarizzato") was
  wrong. The inference is unchanged: an Italian note on a leaf whose other side carries the oblique-shock work.
- **Correction, Mach lines:** the Mach-angle relation sin μ = 1/M was printed by Mach & Salcher in 1887 (the cone
  construction is usually traced to Doppler 1847; not re-read here). 1929 (Prandtl–Busemann) is the date of the
  characteristics method that 71v sets up. This is the weakest of the six items.
- **Weber 1912, read on the text** (Riemann–Weber, 5th ed., vol. II, §206; archive.org `diepartiellendi00riemgoog`):
  "Die … Energiebedingung … ist bei Riemann nicht benutzt, und in der vorigen Auflage dieses Werkes bin ich der
  Riemannschen Darstellung gefolgt. … In der Riemannschen Darstellung ist das Energieprinzip preisgegeben, während
  Hugoniot, Zemplén u. a. die Erhaltung der Entropie fallen lassen." A text search of the volume (OCR) finds no
  Prandtl, wedge, nozzle or two-dimensional steady flow. The editor who had the whole Nachlass in his hands says in
  1912 that Riemann never used the energy condition, and he never printed oblique-shock or expansion theory himself.
- **Classifier, leave-one-volume-out** (`src/writer_id_crossvolume.py`): with no Cod. Ms. 26 page in the Riemann
  training set, 70v → Riemann 0.97 / 1.00 and 71r → 0.97 / 1.00 (two encodings); with no Cod. Ms. 19 page, 3v →

  98 % figure suggests. See `docs/WRITER_ID_RESULTS.md`.
- **Verification scripts:** all re-run, all pass. One line in `verify/r26_70v_71v_crocco_characteristics.py` used to
  print False because sympy could not reduce a difference of two square roots; it now compares the squares.
